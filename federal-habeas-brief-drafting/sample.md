# Sample Sources and Caveats

## Real, Publicly Accessible Sample Filings

**Party-drafted memoranda:**
- Counseled § 2254 Memorandum of Law (M.D. Fla., No. 8:18-cv-00520), ineffective assistance of trial and appellate counsel. Clean BACKGROUND / DISCUSSION / CONCLUSION structure, explicit § 2254(d) framing anchored to *Williams v. Taylor* and *Strickland*, exhaustion-then-merits sequence. https://www.brownstonelaw.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/12/2254-Memorandum-in-Support-of-Writ-of-Habeas-Corpus.pdf — **caveat: a law-firm teaching sample, lighter on record depth than a real capital merits brief; study architecture, not evidentiary density.**
- Washington capital § 2254 petitions (Arntsen; Pirtle, E.D. Wash., No. CT-98-5028-JLQ), reproduced via a FindLaw practitioner overview. Pirtle raises Brady/Napue suppression, prosecutorial misconduct, multi-stage Strickland claims; textbook PARTIES / JURISDICTION / VENUE / PROCEDURAL HISTORY / GROUNDS FOR RELIEF / EXHAUSTION / RELIEF REQUESTED structure, each ground a single all-caps issue tied to a specific constitutional amendment. https://corporate.findlaw.com/litigation-disputes/federal-habeas-corpus-review.html — **caveat: filings are from 1998–99, predate *Richter* (2011) and *Pinholster* (2011); use for structure and issue-framing only, not current AEDPA law.**

**Official USCOURTS opinions reproducing party claim structure:**
- Brady/Napue framing (M.D. Pa., Nos. 3:05-cv-00647/648): three-element Brady framework (favorable evidence / suppression / materiality, per *Banks v. Dretke*) and the Napue false-testimony standard under AEDPA. https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCOURTS-pamd-3_05-cv-00648/pdf/USCOURTS-pamd-3_05-cv-00648-0.pdf
- Schlup/McQuiggin actual-innocence gateway (D. Or., No. 2:13-cv-01612): weighing new-evidence affidavits under "more likely than not that no reasonable juror would have convicted," and delay's effect on credibility. https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCOURTS-ord-2_13-cv-01612/pdf/USCOURTS-ord-2_13-cv-01612-0.pdf
- Additional Brady/Giglio models: N.D. Ala., No. 5:09-cv-00155 (https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCOURTS-alnd-5_09-cv-00155/pdf/USCOURTS-alnd-5_09-cv-00155-0.pdf); E.D. La., No. 2:05-cv-04317 (https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCOURTS-laed-2_05-cv-04317/pdf/USCOURTS-laed-2_05-cv-04317-0.pdf).

## Prose-Style Authority

- Judge J. Nicholas Ranjan (W.D. Pa.) published brief-writing preferences — direct, judge-stated guidance on topic sentences, active voice, avoiding block quotes, cutting adjectives/adverbs: https://www.pawd.uscourts.gov/sites/pawd/files/Ranjan_writing_tips.pdf

## Formatting Sources (Western District of Oklahoma — reverify currency before relying on these)

- WD Okla Local Civil Rules (check for the current version — cited rule numbers: LCvR7.1(e) [type size, margins, page limits], LCvR5.2 [spacing/paper], LCvR3.5 [IFP in habeas actions, PLRA inapplicability]).
- General Order 24-3 (dated July 15, 2024, signed by Chief U.S. District Judge Timothy D. DeGiusti; superseded G.O. 10-1) — capital habeas page limits.
- Court's own habeas filing instructions/approved forms — check the "Prisoner Forms" page on the district's public website.

## Known Limitations of This Guidance

- Local formatting rules (type size, margins, page limits, fee amounts) are **jurisdiction- and time-specific** and are periodically amended. Always confirm current local rules and the assigned judge's individual chambers rules before filing — do not treat any specific number in this skill as permanently fixed.
- The 25-page cap referenced for WD Okla arises from the general motion-practice local rule, which is framed around motions/summary-judgment briefing; habeas merits briefs filed alongside a petition may be treated differently by individual judges. When in doubt, or for a fact-heavy petition, seek leave early rather than assume either way.
- Pro se status does not change the substantive AEDPA/exhaustion/default standards — courts construe pro se filings liberally, but writing a technically sound, professionally voiced brief only increases persuasiveness; it does not forfeit the liberal-construction benefit.
- This skill addresses drafting conventions and general federal habeas doctrine. It is not a substitute for verifying whether any specific claim in a specific case satisfies § 2254(d), is exhausted, or is timely — that always depends on the actual record and the controlling circuit's case law.
